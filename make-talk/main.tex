\begin{frame}{Issues that keep Native Americans from voting}
\vspace{0.5 cm}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.35 cm}
\item<1-> New voter ID requirement in North Dakota.
\begin{itemize}
\item<2-> People without a house number on their tribal ID card are turned away at the polls.
\item<3-> The U.S. mail doesn't deliver to most houses on reservations, so house numbers aren't in common use---most people don't know their full address.
\item<4-> Addresses do exist (maintained by the 911 commissioners), so this is a data access problem that we can do (and are doing) something about.
\end{itemize}
\item<5-> Long distance to polls (up to 80 miles), gas money, time off work, daycare.
\item<6-> Distrust, due to historical disenfranchisement. \\ (Members of Native tribes couldn't vote in North Dakota until 1958!)
\item<7-> Gerrymandering. Recently, the Turtle Mountain Chippewa and Spirit Lake Sioux scored a major victory in overturning a blatant case of gerrymandering:
\begin{itemize}\scriptsize
\item \textcolor{blue}{\url{https://narf.org/cases/north-dakota-redistricting-map/}}

\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{But let's focus on geometric considerations}
\vspace{0.5 cm}

{\bf Polsby-Popper:} The most ``compact,'' least ``salamander'' shape is a circle because a circle has the smallest perimeter for a given area.

\begin{uncoverenv}<2->
\[ \mbox{area of a circle} = A = \pi r^2 \]

\vspace{-0.5 cm}
\[ \mbox{perimeter (circumference) of a circle} = P = 2 \pi r \]
\end{uncoverenv}

\vspace{-0.5 cm}
\[ \uncover<3->{\frac{A}{P^2} =} \uncover<4->{\frac{\pi r^2}{4 \pi^2 r^2} =} \uncover<5->{\frac{1}{4 \pi}} \]

\begin{uncoverenv}<6->
\vspace{0.25 cm}
So if we define

\vspace{-0.1 cm}
\[ \mbox{Polsby-Popper score} = 4 \pi \frac{\mbox{area}}{\mbox{perimeter}^2} \]

\vspace{0.1 cm}
this score is 1 for a circle and less than one for any other shape.
\end{uncoverenv}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{There are others\ldots}
\vspace{0.5 cm}
\begin{itemize}
\item Polsby-Popper: $4 \pi\, \mbox{area} / \mbox{perimeter}^2$
\item Schwartzberg: $2 \pi \sqrt{\mbox{area} / \pi} / \mbox{perimeter}$
\item Reock: $\mbox{area} / \mbox{area of minimum bounding circle}$
\item Box Reock: $\mbox{area} / \mbox{area of minium bounding rectangle}$
\item Convex Hull: $\mbox{area} / \mbox{area of a ``shrink wrapped'' shape around the district}$
\item Skew: $\mbox{area of maximum inscribed circle} / \mbox{area of minimum bounding circle}$
\item Length-Width: $\mbox{width} / \mbox{length}$ of the bounding box
\item X-Symmetry, Y-Symmetry\ldots
\item Boyce-Clark\ldots
\item Fryer-Holden\ldots
\item Edges Removed\ldots
\item Fraction Kept\ldots
\item Log Spanning Tree\ldots
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
